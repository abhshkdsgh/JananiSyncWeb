## 3. Permission, choices, and lawful processing

The healthcare organisation must establish an appropriate legal basis for entering and using patient and informant information, provide the required notice, and obtain consent where required. A staff member's account registration or organisation approval does not give consent on a patient's behalf.

Where consent is required for processing we control, we obtain it before that processing. This policy explains our practices; reading it or continuing to use the app does not by itself supply every required consent. Other processing may take place where applicable law permits or requires it, including qualifying medical emergencies and statutory recordkeeping.

When you submit the account-creation form, a provisional sign-in account is created so your privacy decision can be linked to that account. Organisation and clinical access remain blocked until your acceptance of the current policy has been recorded by the server. You can read this policy before submitting the form. If you deny, access remains blocked and you can sign out. Signing in again does not erase the denial; you will be asked to make a new decision before proceeding. Existing account holders must also record acceptance of the current policy before organisation access.

Acceptance and denial are recorded as separate account-linked events rather than replacing past decisions. Each event identifies the policy version and SHA-256 hash of its complete archived text, the account, the decision, and the server receipt time. Client-reported timing and browser details provide context; they are not independently verified identity evidence. The record shows an action associated with an authenticated account, not proof that a person read every word or consented for any patient. The operator retains decision records to administer access and, where legally necessary, establish or defend claims. Denial does not automatically delete the provisional account or the decision history; you may request deletion or withdrawal by email below.

You may decline to provide information or withdraw consent for consent-based processing by contacting the responsible organisation or our privacy contact. Without information needed for an account or requested function, that function may be unavailable. Withdrawal does not invalidate earlier lawful processing or automatically remove records that must lawfully be retained. Your treating organisation can explain the implications for its records and available care arrangements.

## 4. Who can access information

**Your healthcare organisation.** Administrators can review joining requests, staff identity and role information, membership status, and key-access information. Active administrators, doctors, and nurses with the necessary organisation encryption access can read that organisation's clinical records. The current application does not limit clinical reading to a staff member's assigned patients; access levels primarily govern editing and sign-off capabilities. Membership and encryption access in one organisation do not automatically grant access to another organisation.

**Service providers.** JananiSync uses Google Firebase Authentication for accounts, Cloud Firestore for synchronization and storage, and Firebase App Check with Google reCAPTCHA Enterprise for security. Hosting infrastructure delivers the website and application files. These services process the information necessary for their functions under the applicable service terms. Security and infrastructure information can be processed even before you sign in.

reCAPTCHA Enterprise evaluates browser, device, and interaction-related security signals to detect abuse. It may set a security cookie, including `_GRECAPTCHA`. Its use is for security and abuse prevention. More information is available in [Google's reCAPTCHA documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/recaptcha/docs/faq) and [Firebase's data-processing information](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

**Support and authorised administration.** Authorised personnel may handle account, membership, and operational information needed to support and secure the service. Clinical support should normally use synthetic or redacted examples. If an organisation authorises disclosure of clinical information for a particular support request, access must be limited to that request.

**Legal requirements.** Information may be disclosed to competent authorities or other recipients where required by law or a valid legal order, or where otherwise legally permitted with the necessary authorisation. Any disclosure must be limited to its lawful purpose.

We do not sell personal information or use patient records for targeted advertising. The current application does not send clinical records to a generative AI service. New research, marketing, or other secondary uses require a separate assessment and any notice or authorisation required by law.

## 5. Clinical encryption and its limits

Clinical and roster content is encrypted in the browser before synchronisation to Firestore. Authorised staff decrypt it on their devices using organisation keys. The cloud also stores public-key information, encrypted private-key backups, and encrypted organisation-key envelopes to support access and recovery.

Not everything is encrypted in this way. Account and membership information, organisation and record references, authorship identifiers, access levels, timestamps, revision/confirmation information, and key-management metadata may be readable by the services processing them. These details can reveal relationships and patterns of activity even when clinical content is encrypted.

The application generates and uses recovery phrases locally; its implemented recovery flow does not upload the phrase to Firebase. Keep the phrase confidential. Resetting a sign-in password does not itself recover clinical encryption keys. An administrator may approve replacement access where an authorised organisation key remains available; records may be unrecoverable if all usable copies of the necessary keys are lost.

Encryption does not protect information already displayed on an unlocked device, copied by an authorised person, or exported. Protection also depends on the security of the device, browser, delivered application code, and the integrity of key distribution. It is not a guarantee against a compromised browser, administrator, or service deployment.

## 6. Browser storage, offline use, and exports

JananiSync uses browser storage for account sessions, offline synchronization, and preferences. Firestore's IndexedDB cache can retain encrypted clinical records and readable operational information on the device. The installed web app can cache application files for offline loading. Appearance and greeting settings are stored locally.

If you enable **Remember on this device**, an organisation decryption key is stored in encrypted form together with a local wrapping key in that browser profile. The recovery phrase itself is not saved by this feature. Someone able to use the browser profile or run malicious application code may still be able to use remembered access. Review this setting on each unlock screen and use it only on an appropriately secured device.

Signing out attempts to clear remembered encryption access and clears active application keys. It does not erase every cached record, preference, exported file, or printout. An offline device may not learn that access has been revoked until it reconnects; previously copied keys or information cannot be reliably erased remotely.

You can clear the site's data through browser settings. Before doing so, ensure pending entries have synchronised and necessary recovery phrases are safely available. Clearing local data does not delete the organisation's cloud records. Blocking storage or security cookies may prevent sign-in, offline use, or other functions.

PDF and print previews are generated locally in the browser. Saved or printed charts contain readable patient information and are outside JananiSync's access controls. The exporting organisation must manage recipients, storage, and disposal of those copies. Copying a recovery phrase places it on your device's clipboard, which may be accessible to other software or clipboard history.

## 7. Retention, archiving, and deletion

Personal information is retained according to the purpose and applicable duties for each record category:

| Records | Retention criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Accounts and organisation-access records | While needed to provide or administer the account and organisation access, and thereafter only for applicable legal obligations, security investigations, or establishment or defence of claims |
| Clinical, admission, and associated audit records | For the period the treating organisation needs for care and applicable medical-record, professional, or legal duties; the organisation can explain the period applicable to your records |
| Privacy acceptance and denial history | While needed to administer the account's privacy status and demonstrate the recorded decision, and thereafter only for applicable legal obligations or establishment or defence of claims; requests for erasure are assessed against those purposes |
| Support correspondence | Until the request is resolved and for any further period necessary for related disputes, security, or legal duties |
| Operational/security logs and any backups | For operational recovery, security, and applicable legal requirements; deletion timing also depends on the relevant provider and backup arrangements |

There is no automatic deletion period implemented for these records. Contact the privacy officer for the retention arrangement applicable to your account or organisation. Retention must be reviewed when a purpose ends; an absence of an automatic deletion control does not justify keeping information indefinitely.

Clinical corrections normally add an amendment so the original entry and author remain traceable. Archiving a patient record changes its visibility; it does not erase it. Cancelling a joining request, deactivating access, or leaving an organisation does not automatically delete the associated account, membership history, or clinical contributions.

There is currently no self-service account or patient-record deletion control. Request deletion through section 12 or the responsible healthcare organisation. The request will be assessed against applicable retention duties, clinical record integrity, and any valid legal hold. Where deletion is limited, we will explain the reason and available alternatives. Deleting an authentication account alone does not delete the organisation's clinical record. Where backup copies exist, removal depends on the relevant backup arrangements; the privacy officer can explain applicable limitations. Exports are managed separately by their holders.

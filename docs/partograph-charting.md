# Modified WHO Partograph charting

Sources checked 24 October 2026 for the hospital's requested legacy chart format:

- WHO, *Surgical Care at the District Hospital*, “Using the partograph,” figure 11.5, printed page 11–6: https://indexmedicus.afro.who.int/iah/fulltext/SCDH.pdf
- WHO-hosted *Essential Maternal & Newborn Care Course*, adapted for Pakistan (2011), Module 4, Session 4: https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/operational-guidance/pak-mn-21-operationalguidance-2011-eng-emnc-course-module-1-7.pdf
- WHO Europe, *Promoting Effective Perinatal Care*, Module 11: https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/107482/E79235.pdf

## Recording conventions implemented

- Cervical dilation: X, beginning at 4 cm; every examination at its recorded time column, including the first examination. The action line is parallel and four hours to its right.
- Head descent: O, fifths palpable above the pubic symphysis, 5/5 to 0/5; this is not vaginal station.
- Fetal heart rate and maternal pulse: dots. Blood pressure: paired systolic and diastolic endpoints joined vertically.
- Contractions: count over ten minutes determines the number of boxes. Duration determines shading: below 20 seconds dotted, 20–40 seconds inclusive hatched, above 40 seconds solid. Duration is selected as <20, 20–40 or >40 seconds and stored as a range, without inventing an exact number of seconds. There is no separate strength input or annotation. Older exact-second durations still use the same shading thresholds.
- Liquor: intact, clear, meconium stained, blood stained or absent. Moulding: 0, +, ++, +++.
- Oxytocin: units per litre and drops per minute; drugs/IV fluids are free text. Urine includes protein, acetone and volume.

## Comparison with the supplied paper examples

The chart follows the section order in the five supplied Modified WHO Partograph examples: fetal heart rate; amniotic fluid and moulding; the shared cervical-dilation/descent graph; elapsed hours and actual clock time; contractions; oxytocin; drugs and IV fluids; pulse and BP; temperature; and the three urine rows. The drawing and tap targets use the same layout coordinates.

Contractions shade individual occupied boxes, with their grid boundaries visible above the fill. A legend identifies dotted, hatched and solid duration patterns. BP uses a paired vertical marker with endpoint arrows. Treatment rows have more space for wrapped text; opening their parameter form exposes the full saved text. Displayed negative urine findings use a minus sign while their original stored codes remain unchanged.

Oxytocin has separate displayed concentration and drops-per-minute rows. A recognized entry such as `2.5 U/L · 16 drops/min` displays as `2.5` over `16`; other free text is retained verbatim, and the saved value is never rewritten by display formatting. Text is constrained before the next recorded time so nearby entries do not overlap.

Clock labels appear at each hour, with the chart-start date and date changes at midnight. Every graph shares one origin calculated from valid observations in chronological instant order, including timestamps with different UTC offsets. The first cervical examination does not shift the chart origin or any readings; alert and action lines are reference lines only. The chart's elapsed hours refer to that plotted origin, not automatically to admission time.

The partograph header follows the supplied paper: name, gravida/parity/living children (G/P/L), gestation (POG completed weeks plus 0–6 additional days), hospital number, separate admission date and time, and ruptured-membrane duration in hours. G/P/L and POG have compact combined summaries. LCG retains its original header fields. New values are saved as encrypted header events with cell history; gravida and rupture hours are no longer offered as new time-column observations. A roster-backed patient name is read-only. Historical gravida and rupture-duration values retain their recording time and remain in cell history; they are not silently promoted into admission metadata. POG weeks and additional days are stored as distinct header values. Roster hospital number and admission date/time remain read-only to avoid conflicting copies.

The WHO surgical manual describes half-hourly fetal heart rate, contractions, pulse and oxytocin recordings, two-hourly temperature, four-hourly BP, examination findings at each vaginal examination, and urine measurements when passed. Other WHO-hosted protocols differ in some observation intervals. The interface permits recording actual observations without enforcing a clinical schedule or generating treatment decisions.

## Interaction

LCG and partograph use one explicitly chosen chart start. It defaults to the current time and can be changed to a past date/time before recording. A mandatory setup warning explains that the chosen time establishes the hourly schedule in both views. The first clinical write atomically establishes an immutable chart-start record; subsequent writes must agree with it. Encrypted observations retain the chart start, stable hourly index, and observation time. Existing off-grid legacy columns retain their original times and IDs.

New columns use stable IDs derived from the admission document and hourly index. Empty hourly slots remain visible without creating records. Either view can fill an eligible scheduled hour or backfill a gap without confirming earlier columns. FHR, contraction and oxytocin second-half readings remain attached to their parent hour at +30 minutes. The actual clicked vertical position supplies the reading, never the alert-line position. The selected column is shared across views.

Future observations are blocked separately for each half-hour: having access to the first half does not unlock the second half early. UI and save paths validate times, and Firestore validates the submitted observation timestamp against server time. Scheduled event timestamps must agree with the immutable chart start and column index. Birth marks an endpoint for new ordinary columns; earlier gaps remain eligible for retrospective entry.

First graph click produces a local preview, without a write. The entry overlay or mobile sheet displays the time and readings and provides Confirm and save. Numerical readings use typed inputs with numeric/decimal mobile keyboards, visible allowed limits, and validation that disables saving outside those limits. BP also requires systolic above diastolic. Duration ranges and categorical findings retain their selection controls. This is the mark confirmation; there is no additional dialog. Further graph taps adjust the preview without saving. Changing position or parameters changes only the preview. Choosing head descent in an entry card also selects Descent O for graph taps, so a subsequent tap cannot switch the draft back to cervical dilation. Both descent and cervical dilation use the selected time column, independently of the alert line. Contraction and BP parameters are selected in multi-column overlays. Cancel discards the preview. Tapping anywhere inside a time band targets that column, without requiring a tap on its grid line or saved symbol. Ordinary readings use whole-hour bands; FHR, contractions and oxytocin use half-hour bands. The clicked vertical position supplies the new numeric value, while related values (such as BP partner or contraction duration) remain prefilled. Existing columns retain their exact recorded times, including off-grid and second-half readings. Text rows prefill their saved contents. Open-column edits use the same per-cell access rules as LCG. Confirmed marks remain directly editable according to their per-cell access levels. An explicit correction dialog remains available when a reasoned correction is desired; its authority is also determined per cell. A complete mark is one encrypted event, so frequency/duration and paired BP values are committed together and remain separately discoverable in cell history. Access checks apply to each edited cell: a higher-access edit protects that cell while other cells at the same time point remain editable. Each half-hour entry has its own access level. A grouped graph mark requires access to every parameter it changes. Confirmation does not lock cells. Both ordinary edits and explicit corrections require access at least equal to each affected cell’s recorded access level; there is no additional confirmer-based threshold. Explicit corrections require a reason. Every write appends revisions to the affected cells’ histories. The column remains confirmed and its original confirmation is retained. Clinical staff can confirm columns without a highest-editor restriction. Confirmation is an audit acknowledgement, not a prerequisite for further entry.

Partograph and LCG share the same admission time columns. Saving a mark does not automatically confirm its column, and neither saving nor confirmation blocks another eligible scheduled column. Fetal heart rate and contractions at the column time and 30 minutes later remain in that column as first- and second-half readings. Column status and per-cell access are checked before preview and again when saving. The ready, open and selected columns are highlighted across the full hour width, including both half-hour subcolumns. Selecting a second-half reading keeps its parent column highlighted. Column confirmation buttons appear only beneath the visible open columns; a single card above the chart provides a column selector, a parameter selector and one Record parameter button, without confirmation controls. Only elapsed time columns are offered; second-half parameters appear only once their own observation time is available. Existing confirmed columns remain selectable under per-cell access rules. Legacy records are never automatically merged, retimestamped, or confirmed. Explicitly selecting a legacy column takes priority when several share one visual time band.

The shared entry card opens a parameter form at the selected column’s exact recorded time. This supports linked LCG columns recorded between the chart's half-hour grid positions without rounding or changing their timestamps. FHR and contraction forms can target the second half-hour of that same column; the preview displays the actual observation time, including date rollover. Existing values are prefilled; missing measurements remain blank and must be selected before saving.

The chart renders at twice its former default size: cells, labels, marks, and linework all scale together, giving approximately 157 dp per hour and 18–22 sp chart text at default font settings. Later columns remain accessible by horizontal scrolling rather than shrinking the chart to fit. The graph supports 60–300% zoom relative to this enlarged default, with explicit 60%, 75%, 90%, and 100% steps using two-finger pinch gestures and visible zoom controls. Drawing and hit testing share the zoom factor; single-finger swipes continue to scroll. The entry cards remain available above the chart.

Wide layouts show the parameter form as an overlay within the chart, keeping the grid in place; phones use a bottom sheet. An open form keeps its presentation until saved or cancelled, including when the window is resized, to avoid replacing its UI layer during adaptive measurement.

These conventions describe the supplied Modified WHO Partograph; LCG's existing recording interface remains unchanged.

Eligible confirmed columns display a pencil icon in the column heading (at the corresponding time position on the partograph). The icon opens the required-reason correction form; compact layouts place it beside the selected column title. Eligibility is determined by per-cell access, independently of the original confirmer’s access level.

Oxytocin supports separate first-half and +30-minute readings in each hourly column. Each half has its own access level and history; legacy hourly oxytocin entries remain visible in the first half only. Both the U/L and drops/min display rows use the selected half-hour. The second half is unavailable until its own observation time, including server-side timestamp validation.

All plotted partograph observations except drugs, IV fluids, and urine rows support separate first-half and +30-minute entries. This includes amniotic fluid, moulding, cervical dilation, descent, pulse, paired BP, temperature, FHR, contractions, and oxytocin. The LCG retains only its original half-hour fields (FHR, decelerations, contraction frequency and duration). Its other rows consume the first-half value only; additional second-half observations remain stored for partograph display and history, never substituted into the LCG's hourly reading. First-half cell keys and edit permissions remain compatible with existing records.

Saved values can be erased from the parameter form using Erase saved value and Confirm erase in the same overlay. Erasure appends an explicit empty cell event and retains its history and access protection. For paired BP and contraction fields, only the chosen field is cleared. Other readings and the opposite half-hour are preserved. Explicit first-half clears remain blank in LCG as well as partograph, rather than being interpreted as numeric zero.

Both chart headers autosave valid typed edits on focus loss and commit categorical/date-time selections immediately. Field history is available through a trailing history icon, including alongside the date picker. Header writes are serialized so moving quickly among fields preserves all edits, deduplicates unchanged values, and scopes queued saves to the original admission and organization. Partograph numerical entry fields include trailing +1/−1 arrowheads with touch targets and bound checks; decimal steps preserve the fractional digits.

On compact screens, LCG remains a table rather than a stacked form. Section, observation and alert columns stay visible on the left; exactly one time column (including its original half-hour subdivisions) is displayed on the right. Previous/next arrowheads in the sticky time heading page the data while retaining vertical scroll position. Mobile paging skips presentation columns marked Skipped after full dilation; those columns remain visible in the wide chart and PDF export. Confirmation and stage-action rows retain their height across recorded and empty columns so paging near the bottom does not shorten the table or pull it upward. The phase and column headings remain frozen together. Cell editors, history badges, corrections, confirmation and labour-progress X/O/P marks share the desktop behavior. Finishing an autosave in a previous column does not override a newer column selection.

## Integrity and stage controls

The Partograph now exposes the same full-dilation/skip-to-second-stage and mark-birth confirmation actions as LCG. Both interfaces use the same stage-aware column schedule, including off-hour transitions and retrospective transitions that share a timestamp with the preceding column. Column identity includes its index; no existing column is retimestamped. New encrypted events store only declared cells, and readers reconstruct current values from authorized deltas.
